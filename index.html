Research Overview

This research investigates how early-stage ventures in low-access capital markets can strategically leverage intermediaries to overcome structural barriers to venture capital funding.

The Atlanta Puzzle: Despite being a top-tier U.S. city with Fortune 500 headquarters and world-class universities, Atlanta has a 15-to-1 venture capital deficit compared to San Francisco, leaving talented entrepreneurs struggling to secure investor meetings.

Core Research Questions

  • Founder Strategy: How should founders structure their intermediary portfolio to secure meetings and funding?
  • Funder Perspective: What barriers prevent investors from deploying capital in markets like Atlanta?

Theoretical Frameworks

Methodology

Mixed-methods approach combining:

  • Quantitative: Regression models analyzing intermediary portfolio effects on funding speed and success
  • Qualitative: Semi-structured investor interviews examining market perception and barriers

Theoretical Framework

This study integrates four major theories into a unified framework:

  • Network Theory (Burt, 1992) - Structural holes and bridging disconnected networks
  • Broker Intermediation (Hallen & Eisenhardt, 2012) - Catalyzing ties through warm introductions
  • Resource Dependence Theory (Pfeffer & Salancik, 1978) - Concentration risk in resource access
  • Absorptive Capacity (Cohen & Levinthal, 1990) - Converting access into actionable outcomes

Key Hypotheses

  • H1a - Wide Net Hypothesis: Greater intermediary diversity accelerates access and increases funding success
  • H1b - Concentration Trap Hypothesis: High concentration in single intermediary type slows access and reduces success
  • H2 - Preparedness Moderation: Founder preparedness amplifies portfolio strategy effects
  • H3 - Signal-and-Search: Investors face signaling and search cost barriers in low-access markets
